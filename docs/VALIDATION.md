### Three example false positives

**1. P00664: score 56 (Medium)**

- $600.00 deposited in 30 days vs $66.67 per 30 days over the previous 90 (9.0x).
- Deposits in 30 days: $600.00 vs $60.00 typical for players who usually bet like them (regular, low-stake bettors, 1192 players; z = 3.4).
- 38% of bets placed between midnight and 6 a.m., up from 6% over the previous 90 days.

Recommended action: Safer Gambling check-in.

Why it is a false positive: this is a normal player planted with a busy month (more betting days and 1.5-2x stakes in the last 30 days, 'benign surge'). The own-baseline rules correctly see the change but cannot know it is harmless; a light-touch Safer Gambling check-in is still proportionate.

**2. P00780: score 50 (Medium)**

- Deposits in 30 days: $9,260.00 vs peer median $540.00, 17.1x the peer median (z = 3.3, peer group 35-44 | 120k+ | Toronto).
- 3 withdrawal(s) totalling $5,887.68 went to a different payment method than the deposit they followed (within 7 days).

Recommended action: Review play against the declared profile; request source of funds if unexplained.

Why it is a false positive: this is a high earner planted with a steady history of large deposits ('steady high value'). Deposits stay inside the income band; the flags come from the comparison with peers plus ordinary month-to-month swings in losses.

**3. P01830: score 47 (Medium)**

- Average stake: $281.29 vs peer median $47.02, 6.0x the peer median (z = 3.4, peer group 25-34 | 80-120k).
- A gradient-boosting model trained on labelled risk scenarios rates this player's pattern 88% likely to match known risky behaviour (scored without seeing this player). Biggest drivers (SHAP): deposits vs income 0.7x; average stake vs peers z = 3.4; average stake $281.29.

Recommended action: Review play against the declared profile; request source of funds if unexplained.

Why it is a false positive: this is a high-income player planted with stakes 3-5x their peers' for the whole period ('high roller'). No risky behaviour was injected; the flags come from that steady high-stakes play plus ordinary month-to-month swings in deposits and losses.

## Prioritisation: does the score separate the top of the queue?

The score is the sum of reason points with two adjustments: flags of the same anomaly type overlap, so within a type the strongest counts in full and the rest at half; and above 70 points each extra point counts progressively less (a soft cap), so very strong cases stay ordered instead of all reading 100. Equal scores are ordered by total points, then the AI model's probability.

Alerts use **46** distinct scores (highest 97); the largest group sharing one score is **30** alerts, and **11** once total points break ties.

## AI model (supervised)

A gradient-boosting classifier (scikit-learn `HistGradientBoostingClassifier`) learns which feature combinations match the labelled scenarios; SHAP explains each player's score. Every player is scored by a model that never saw them (5-fold out-of-fold), and the result enters the score as one more reason (`ML_MODEL`: +10 at probability 0.5, +20 at 0.85), so it is explained like every other flag.

Out-of-fold ROC-AUC **0.998**, average precision **0.994**, recall at probability 0.5 **96%** with **0.06%** of normal players above 0.5.

Most influential features (mean |SHAP|): deposits vs income, betting turnover vs deposits, average stake vs peers, deposits vs behaviour peers, declared vs occupation income, average stake vs behaviour peers.

## Thresholds and weights in use

Bands: Low 0-39, Medium 40-69, High 70-89, Critical 90-99 (alert at 40+). Rule thresholds and points live in `THRESHOLDS` and `build_reasons()` in `backend/pipeline/detect.py`; the peer z threshold is 3.0 (upward deviations only, robust z on a log scale). Compliance indicators use the $10,000 24-hour total (`LARGE_TRANSACTION_24H`) and deposits of $8,000-$9,999.99 or split deposits crossing $10,000 (`STRUCTURING`).

## Known limits

- The data and the detectors were designed by the same team, so these numbers show the pipeline works end to end; they are not evidence of real-world performance.
- The legacy-rules comparison depends on how many legitimate high-value players the generator plants (`steady_high_value`, 1% of normal players). These are the players fixed "deposit over $X" rules mis-rank; with none of them, both orderings tie at the top.
- Peer deviation is the hardest type: a single metric far above peers scores 25-40 points on its own, so some injected players stay just under the alert threshold. Players who barely bet in the current window cannot be caught by 30-day features at all.
- The AI model learns the planted scenarios, so its scores here are optimistic even out-of-fold: it has seen other players of the same scenario. In production it would be trained on investigator-confirmed outcomes and monitored for drift.
